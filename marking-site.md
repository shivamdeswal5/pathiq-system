# LabOS — Premium Product Website Creative Brief

## Project

Design and build a premium, cinematic, conversion-focused marketing website for LabOS, a laboratory operations platform developed for independent diagnostic labs.

LabOS helps small pathology labs move from paper-based operations to digital workflows. It supports sample registration, report generation, referral tracking, outsourced testing workflows, and lab business management.

The product's long-term vision is to become the operating system for independent diagnostic labs, starting in India and expanding internationally.

## The central idea

**Your lab deserves better than paper.**

The website must communicate a meaningful transformation: a small independent diagnostic lab can become a modern, digitally managed business without needing an IT department or a complicated enterprise implementation.

The visitor should feel:

"This product understands my lab, it looks trustworthy, and I can actually see myself using it."

## Creative direction

Create a distinctive, premium technology brand that combines:

- Cinematic storytelling inspired by high-quality game launch websites.
- Editorial typography and polished product presentation inspired by Apple.
- Experimental scroll interactions and sophisticated motion design.
- The clarity, trust, and usability expected from healthcare software.

Do not imitate an existing website. Create an original visual identity and experience for LabOS.

## Visual identity

Primary background: deep midnight navy, approximately #101D2D.

Primary accent: intelligent teal/aquamarine, approximately #17C9B0.

Secondary background: warm white, approximately #F4F5F2.

Typography: Manrope for display headlines, Inter for body text and UI, and IBM Plex Mono for occasional technical details.

Use generous whitespace, large typography, precise alignment, restrained gradients, subtle borders, and sophisticated visual hierarchy.

The design must feel premium and international without becoming intimidating to a small diagnostic lab owner.

## Website experience

Build the homepage as a visual story:

1. A cinematic hero introducing LabOS and the transformation from paper to digital.
2. A scroll-driven demonstration of the lab workflow.
3. A detailed product showcase using realistic software interfaces.
4. A business intelligence section showing the value of understanding costs, referrals, and profitability.
5. A growth section showing how one lab can expand to multiple branches.
6. Transparent pricing, onboarding information, and a strong final conversion section.

## Product presentation

The actual product must be the hero of the website.

Use realistic browser mockups, authentic product screenshots, and a clear visual demonstration of the software.

Show the workflow from sample registration to report generation and report delivery.

Show how referral tracking and outsourced testing work.

Show the lab owner's dashboard and the ability to understand the business beyond individual reports.

Do not invent features, customer testimonials, or product capabilities.

## Motion and interaction

Create a cinematic but lightweight experience.

Use scroll-driven transitions, subtle parallax, sticky product demonstrations, smooth interface state changes, and carefully timed typography animations.

One signature interaction should visually transform a paper-based workflow into a digital LabOS workflow.

Use motion to explain the product, not simply to decorate the page.

The website must remain fast, accessible, and usable on mobile devices. Respect reduced-motion preferences and provide appropriate mobile alternatives to complex desktop animations.

## Conversion

The primary CTA should be "See how it works."

The secondary CTA should be "View pricing."

Make onboarding, pricing, and the next step clear.

The site should be convincing through real product demonstrations, understandable benefits, and transparent information rather than exaggerated marketing claims.

## Avoid

Generic AI-generated SaaS layouts.

Purple gradients, random floating glass cards, meaningless 3D spheres, and generic medical stock imagery.

Repeated feature cards with identical layouts.

Excessive animation that delays access to the product.

Fake dashboards, fake testimonials, invented customer logos, and unsupported statistics.

## Final outcome

The result should feel like a premium product launch for a serious technology company.

A visitor should understand what LabOS does, see how it works, understand why it matters to their lab, and know exactly how to get started.

The website should be visually memorable, product-focused, technically excellent, and credible enough to present to international healthcare customers and investors.

## Important: Existing Codebase & Product Accuracy

This is an existing product, not a new project. Before making any changes, inspect the current codebase and the `docs/` folder thoroughly.

- Review the existing implementation, documentation, and product features to understand exactly what has been built and what is currently functional.
- Use the `docs/` folder as the primary reference for implemented features, product capabilities, and business requirements. Cross-check with the actual code wherever necessary.
- Keep all marketing content, product demonstrations, screenshots, and feature claims strictly aligned with the existing implementation.
- Do not invent features, workflows, integrations, statistics, or capabilities that are not implemented. Clearly distinguish future plans from existing functionality.
- Preserve the existing architecture, framework, dependencies, routing, and working functionality. Do not rebuild the project unnecessarily.

### Design & Implementation

The goal is to transform the existing website into a premium, cinematic, product-focused experience while keeping the actual product realistic.

Use the existing product UI and real implemented workflows wherever possible. If a feature is incomplete or unavailable, do not create a fake interactive demo that suggests it is functional.

First, understand what already exists. Then improve the design, storytelling, animations, and presentation around the actual product.

The final website must be visually impressive, technically sound, responsive, and production-ready. Every visual interaction should serve a purpose, and every product claim should be backed by the codebase or documentation.

**Build around what LabOS actually is today, while presenting its future vision as an ambition—not an existing capability.**
